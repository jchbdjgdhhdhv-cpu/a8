The Pan Sauce Guide

Every time you sear meat, fish or mushrooms, the pan holds the start of a sauce. Five minutes and a few ingredients turn it into something special.

Mise en place

  • A heavy pan (not non-stick)
  • 250 ml good stock
  • 1 shallot, finely chopped
  • 1–2 tbsp cold butter
  • Salt, pepper, lemon

  1. Sear and rest

    Cook your steak, chicken, chops or fish in a hot pan until well browned. Move it to a warm plate to rest. Look at the pan: those brown bits are called the fond.

  2. Soften the aromatics

    Pour off all but a spoonful of fat. Lower the heat and cook a chopped shallot or garlic for a minute until soft, without burning the fond.

  3. Deglaze

    Pour in about 250 ml of stock and scrape the bottom with a wooden spoon so the browned bits dissolve into the liquid. A splash of lemon juice or a spoon of vinegar can add brightness.

  4. Reduce

    Simmer until reduced by about half and the sauce lightly coats a spoon. Add any juices from the resting plate.

  5. Finish off the heat

    Take the pan off the heat and swirl in cold butter, a spoon of mustard, cream or chopped herbs. Season with salt, pepper and lemon. Spoon over and serve straight away.
