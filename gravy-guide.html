The Gravy Guide

Good gravy is smooth, glossy and full of flavour. Whether you start with roasting juices or just stock, the method is the same.

Mise en place

  • Roasting tin juices or 3 tbsp butter
  • 3 tbsp plain flour
  • 500 ml hot stock
  • Salt and pepper
  • A whisk

  1. Collect the drippings

    Pour roasting juices into a jug and let the fat rise. Spoon about 3 tablespoons of fat back into the tin or a pan; keep the dark juices underneath for flavour. No roast? Use butter.

  2. Make the roux

    Over medium heat, whisk 3 tablespoons of flour into the fat. Cook for 2–3 minutes, stirring, until it smells nutty and turns light brown.

  3. Add hot stock gradually

    Pour in hot stock a little at a time, whisking constantly. Adding it slowly is the secret to no lumps. Scrape up the browned bits as you go and add the reserved juices.

  4. Simmer

    Simmer for 5 minutes until thickened and glossy. Too thick, add stock; too thin, simmer longer. Use the roux calculator to scale it.

  5. Season and strain

    Taste and season. For the smoothest gravy, pour it through a sieve into a warm jug. Keep warm over very low heat and whisk before serving.
